App Blockers Without a Subscription

Searching "free app blocker" on the App Store returns a wall of apps that are free to download and $6.99 a week to actually use. Here's why that happens, how to spot it before you install, and what your genuinely no-subscription options are.

Disclosure: this page is published by SproutGuard, which is one of the apps discussed. We've tried to be specific about where the built-in free option beats us — because for a lot of people it does.

Why almost every blocker charges monthly

It isn't only greed. It's architecture. If an app has accounts, cross-device sync, a web dashboard, "insights", or an accountability partner feature, it is running servers — and servers bill every month whether or not you open the app. Recurring cost forces recurring revenue.

The flip side is the useful part: a blocker that has no server has no monthly cost to cover. Since iOS 16, Apple's Family Controls / Managed Settings APIs let a third-party app perform real system-level blocking entirely on-device. An app built that way needs no account, stores nothing remotely, and can therefore give away the core function without bleeding money.

How to spot a fake free tier in 60 seconds

  1. Read the In-App Purchases list on the App Store page. Weekly pricing is the tell — weekly plans exist to convert a 3-day trial before you remember it.
  2. Install and try to complete one block. If you can't finish a single block without payment details, blocking is the paid feature.
  3. Check whether it demands an account. An account on a screen time app means your usage patterns leave your phone. Apple's API doesn't require that — it's a product choice.
  4. Search the reviews for "trial" and "charged." That's where the paywall behaviour is documented honestly.

Honest recommendation: before installing anything, set a Screen Time passcode and one Downtime window. It's free and it fixes the problem for a meaningful number of people. If you do that and still find yourself back in the app every day, the missing piece is motivation structure — a session you can't argue with and a streak you don't want to reset — and that's when a third-party blocker earns its place.
