How to Turn Off “Ignore Limit” on iPhone Screen Time

Short answer: you cannot remove the button itself. What you can do is make it stop being a one-tap escape. For most people that means a Screen Time passcode plus one Downtime window in the hours where they actually lose control.

Worth knowing up front: this can be done free with Apple’s built-in Screen Time, and the steps are below. If you want the same system-level block wrapped in a session and a streak, SproutGuard does that on-device with no account.

Why the button exists at all

Apple designed App Limits as a self-control feature, not a jail. So when the limit fires, iOS still offers an override path. The problem is not technical. The problem is timing: the override question appears at the exact moment your judgement is worst and relief is one tap away.

That is why telling yourself to “just not press it” usually fails. You need the decision to happen earlier, when you are calm, not inside the craving.

The setup that makes Ignore Limit stop helping

1. Set a Screen Time passcode you do not have ready

Go to Settings > Screen Time > Lock Screen Time Settings. Use four digits you do not already use elsewhere. Best case: generate them, write them down, and put the paper somewhere inconvenient enough that reaching it takes a minute or two.

This is the real switch. Without a passcode, Screen Time is advisory. With a passcode you do not know by reflex, the override becomes a small trip instead of a reflex tap.

2. Turn on Downtime with blocking enabled

Go to Settings > Screen Time > Downtime, enable a schedule, and make sure the block toggle is on. During that window, blocked apps ask for the passcode instead of giving you the cheap version of “Ignore Limit”.

If your danger window is bedtime scrolling, schedule the block for bedtime. If it is the first hour after waking, schedule it there. A badly targeted window feels oppressive and gets deleted. A narrow, accurate one survives.

3. Put the block where the relapse actually happens

Most people do not need all-day lockdown. They need one or two recurring windows covered well. Look at the last three times you broke the limit. If they cluster around 10pm–12am, build the rule there. If they cluster around lunch or the commute, use that instead.

4. Close the escape route your habit uses next

If the app is blocked, the habit often moves instead of stopping.

  • Browser version: add the site under Content & Privacy Restrictions > Content Restrictions > Web Content > Never Allow.
  • Reinstalling: set Content & Privacy Restrictions > iTunes & App Store Purchases > Installing Apps to Don't Allow.
  • Deleting the limit: the passcode above is what stops this.

When built-in Screen Time still is not enough

If you already did all of the above and still override it, the missing piece usually is not a stricter lock. It is a visible cost to relapsing. Apple gives you restrictions and reports. It does not give you much motivation structure.
