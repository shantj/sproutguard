Best App Blockers for Adults on iPhone

Search "app blocker" and about eighty percent of the results are parental control software wearing a different hat. If you're an adult trying to block yourself, that's the wrong category — and the difference isn't cosmetic.

Disclosure before you read the comparison: I develop one of the apps listed, SproutGuard. The comparison below includes options that beat it on specific points, and says so.

Why the category matters

Parental control apps are architected around two people: a supervisor who sets the rules and sees the reports, and a subject who lives under them. That design decision cascades. It means an account, so the two devices can be paired. It means a server, so reports can travel. It means your usage data leaves your phone, because the entire point is that someone else can see it.

An adult self-control tool has one person in the loop. You are both the supervisor and the subject. Nothing needs to travel anywhere, because there's nobody to report to. Which means the honest version of this app needs no account, no server, and no data collection at all.

The one-question filter: does it require an account? If yes, ask what the account is for. For a single-user on-device blocker, the answer is usually "so we have your data," not "so the app works."

What's actually available

iOS Screen Time (free, built in)

The baseline, and better than its reputation — if you set the Screen Time passcode. Without one, every block screen hands you an "Ignore Limit" button and the whole system collapses. With one, App Limits and Downtime are genuinely effective.

Weak spot: no streaks, no sessions, and reports you stop reading after week two. It measures; it doesn't motivate.

Dedicated focus/blocker apps (mixed)

A crowded shelf. The useful ones share three traits: they build on Apple's Screen Time API rather than fighting it, they make blocks hard to dismiss mid-session, and they give you a progress signal stronger than a bar chart. Watch for two common problems — subscriptions in the $50–80/year range for what is fundamentally a toggle, and mandatory accounts on an app that has no technical need for one.

Friction apps (delay, don't block)

A different philosophy: instead of blocking, they insert a breathing pause before the app opens. Genuinely effective for reflex scrolling and much gentler. Less effective when the pull is strong — a pause you can wait out is a pause you'll wait out.

  • Never tried the built-in properly? Set a Screen Time passcode first. Free, and it fixes more cases than people expect.
