How to Block TikTok on iPhone

Most guides list the same four taps and stop. The interesting part is what happens on day three, when the block is technically still on and you're somehow watching TikTok anyway. Here's the full method plus the four loopholes that undo it.

Short on time? The steps below are free and work without any extra app. If you would rather not maintain the settings yourself, SproutGuard applies the same system-level block in one tap and adds a streak so relapsing has a visible cost. Free, no account.

The fastest method (2 minutes)

  1. Open Settings > Screen Time > App Limits > Add Limit.
  2. Choose Social, then tap Edit Apps and select only TikTok — otherwise you'll block messaging too and delete the limit out of frustration by Tuesday.
  3. Set the limit to 1 minute. Not zero — the minimum is one minute, and that's effectively a block.
  4. Set Lock Screen Time Settings with a passcode. Skip this and the whole thing is decorative.

The four loopholes

1. tiktok.com in the browser

App Limits apply to the app binary, not the website. The web player works, and this is the single most common way a TikTok block quietly dies. Fix: Content & Privacy Restrictions > Content Restrictions > Web Content > Limit Adult Websites, then add tiktok.com under Never Allow. Add www.tiktok.com and vm.tiktok.com separately — the short-link domain is what friends actually send you.

2. Ignore Limit

When the limit triggers you get a one-tap escape. It's not removable, but you can neutralise it: put a Downtime window over your danger hours and turn on the block toggle. Apps blocked by Downtime demand the passcode rather than offering an easy override.

3. Reinstalling

Deleting TikTok is the block people try first and it lasts about a day, because redownloading a previously installed app takes fifteen seconds and your session is usually restored. Fix: Content & Privacy Restrictions > iTunes & App Store Purchases > Installing Apps > Don't Allow.

4. In-app browsers

A TikTok link tapped inside a messaging app may open in that app's embedded browser, which doesn't always inherit the same restrictions. There's no clean toggle for this. The practical mitigation is the web-content block above, which applies broadly, plus the habit of not opening video links from chat at all.

The honest version: Apple's free built-in tools can block TikTok completely. What they can't do is make you want to keep the block. That's a motivation problem, not a technical one — and it's why a visible streak, something you lose by overriding, tends to outlast pure restriction.

Set a replacement, not just a wall

TikTok fills a specific slot: seconds of dead time with no cognitive load. Block it without putting something in that slot and you'll migrate to Reels or Shorts within a week — the same behaviour with a different logo. Decide in advance what occupies that slot, and make it require no setup. A downloaded podcast, a book on the phone, an actual walk. The blocking is the easy half.
