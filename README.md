## Why this exists

Most screen time apps are parental control tools wearing a productivity costume. They assume someone else needs to police you. Real reviews of the leading "self-control" apps read like this:

> *"My parents installed this. I cannot use the internet without fear of being monitored."*

The rest want an account, a subscription, and your usage data sitting on their servers.

SproutGuard assumes the opposite: **you're an adult who already knows the problem.** You just want a wall — and you want to be the one who builds it.

## What it does

- **Block what eats your day** — pick the exact apps and websites that pull you in
- **Focus sessions** — start a session and stay locked in until you're done
- **Streaks** — build a daily streak; watching the number climb is what makes it stick
- **Usage insights** — see your real screen time and watch it fall week over week

## FAQ

**Is this a parental control app?**
No — that's the whole point. It's designed for you to block yourself. No monitoring dashboard, no reports, no second party.

**Do I need an account?**
No. There is no sign-up, no login, and no backend.

**How does blocking work?**
It's built on Apple's own Screen Time framework, so blocking happens at the system level, entirely on-device.

**Is it free?**
Yes. Blocking, focus sessions, and streaks are free. There's an optional Pro upgrade for power features.

**What if I just turn it off?**
You can — it's your phone. The streak is the point: friction plus a number you don't want to reset is what actually changes behavior.
