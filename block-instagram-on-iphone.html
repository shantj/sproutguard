How to Block Instagram on iPhone

Blocking the app takes about forty seconds. Making the block survive contact with your own willpower is the part every tutorial skips — so this one starts with the three loopholes.

Short on time? Everything below is free and needs no extra app. If you would rather not hand-configure it, SproutGuard sets the same system-level block in one tap and tracks a streak. Free, no account, nothing leaves your phone.

First: the three things that undo an Instagram block

  1. Safari. You block the app; instagram.com still loads in a browser. This is the number one failure.
  2. "Ignore Limit." Without a Screen Time passcode, the block screen has a button that gives you the rest of the day back for free.
  3. Reinstalling. Deleting the app is not a block. It is a fifteen-second speed bump, and the login is still saved.

Any method below that doesn't close all three will fail within a week. That's not a willpower problem; it's a configuration problem.

1. Block the Instagram app with a Screen Time App Limit

  1. Settings → Screen Time → App Limits → Add Limit
  2. Social → tap the arrow → tick Instagram only (leave the rest of Social alone unless you mean it)
  3. Set the time to 1 minute — not 30. A 30-minute budget is permission, not a block.
  4. Tap Add

From now on Instagram greys out roughly the moment you open it.

2. Block instagram.com in Safari (the step everyone misses)

  1. Settings → Screen Time → Content & Privacy Restrictions → turn it on
  2. Content Restrictions → Web Content → Limit Adult Websites
  3. Under Never Allow, tap Add Website and enter instagram.com

Do this for every browser-reachable surface you actually use. If you only block the app, you have moved your habit, not removed it.

3. Close the "Ignore Limit" loophole

Settings → Screen Time → Lock Screen Time Settings. Set a four-digit passcode that isn't your phone passcode and isn't a date you can guess in a distracted moment. Ideally have someone else type it, or generate one and don't look.

This single step is the difference between Screen Time working and not working. Everything above is advisory until a passcode stands behind it. If you skip it, you'll be back inside Instagram tonight and you'll conclude that blockers don't work for you. They didn't get a chance to.

4. Add Downtime for the two windows that matter

Settings → Screen Time → Downtime → Scheduled. The two windows where Instagram does the most damage for most people are the first thirty minutes after waking and the last hour before sleep. Set 10pm–8am and strip "Always Allowed" down to Phone and Messages.

When the built-ins still aren't enough

Apple's tools are budget-shaped: they count minutes and then argue with you. If you've already set all of the above and still find yourself in the app, the missing piece usually isn't a stricter limit — it's state you don't want to break.
